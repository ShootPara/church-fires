Red Hot Flue Slide: In this article from March 24, 1926, we learn about the official investigation into the tragic fire that destroyed the First Methodist Church in Kalamazoo on March 13. The fire not only caused over $100,000 in property damage but also claimed the lives of two brave firefighters. An investigating committee comprised of Fire Chief **Charles H. Russell**, Sheriff **Jerome S. Borden**, Acting Police Chief **Roy W. Carney**, and contractor **Frank Pfaltz** conducted a thorough examination of the church's ruins to determine the cause of the blaze.

Their investigation concluded that the fire originated from an **overheated furnace and flue** located in the basement directly beneath the church organ. Evidence showed that the flue was still a rosy red color, indicating it had been red hot, and the floor joists above were charred to charcoal. This overheating set fire to the floor joists, and the flames quickly spread upward through a partition to the roof. The presence of wooden shingles beneath the newer roof covering provided additional fuel, allowing the fire to spread rapidly.

Sheriff Borden noted that all four furnaces in the church basement had been well filled and "banked" with coal, suggesting they were heavily loaded and left to burn slowly over time. Importantly, the committee found that the electrical wiring in the building was not a contributing factor to the fire.

This report brings closure to the questions surrounding the fire's origin, attributing it to an overheated heating system rather than faulty wiring or arson. It serves as a somber reminder of the importance of proper furnace maintenance and monitoring to prevent such devastating incidents in the future. The findings also underscore the risks faced by firefighters and the critical need for safety measures in public buildings.
