WEDNESDAY, MARCH 24, 1926

RED HOT FLUE RAZED CHURCH, PROBERS FIND
Overheated Furnace Blamed for Destruction of Methodist Edifice.

That the First Methodist church fire March 13 started from an overheated furnace and flue, was the report of an official investigating committee composed of Fire Chief Charles H. Russell, Sheriff Jerome S. Borden, Acting Police Chief Roy W. Carney, and Frank Pfaltz, contractor, Tuesday.

A thorough investigation of the ruins at the church site was conducted by this committee in a determined effort to learn the origin of the fire which claimed the lives of two firemen and wrought property damage estimated above $100,000.

MAKE THOROUGH PROBE
Each of the four furnaces in the church basement, all of the flue lines, and all electrical wiring that could be traced, were examined by the committee and at the conclusion of the inspection there remained but one plausible theory as to the origin of the fire. Evidence found in and around the furnace which stood in the basement directly beneath the church organ, led the committee to believe the heating unit had become dangerously hot, setting fire to the floor joists.

The flue to the furnace in question is at present a rosy red color, indicating that it had been red hot. Other flues are still shiny and show no evidence of having been overheated. The joists and floor above the furnace are charcoal, while the joists in other parts of the first floor are only smoke and soot-stained.

SET FIRE TO JOISTS.
The investigating committee is certain the fire started in the floor joists above the furnace near the rear of the basement, eating through the floor and coursing up a partition to the roof. Wooden shingles, said to have been beneath the newer roof covering, furnished fuel to the rapidly spreading flames.

Sheriff Borden, who announced the committee's report, stated that all four of the furnaces had been well filled with coal and had evidently been "banked" with coal. Electrical wiring units in the building, as many as could be inspected, were found in such a condition as not to produce fire.
