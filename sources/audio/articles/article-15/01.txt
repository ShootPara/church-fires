WEDNESDAY, DECEMBER 30, 1925

FIRE ORIGIN UNKNOWN
While statements of Arthur Stoops and John Triestram, the two boys who discovered the fire, and those of firemen first on the scene seem to indicate an overheated furnace was the cause of the conflagration, the Rev. Phelps reports that the blaze is considered of unknown origin. Claude S. Price, janitor of the church building, stated that there was very little fire in the furnace, according to his knowledge, Tuesday.

Stoops and Triestram, who had been playing in the boys' department of the Y.M.C.A. building, left there about 12:25 p.m., going through the alley between the "Y" and the church toward the rear of the latter and Academy Street. Smoke could be seen coming from beneath a rear door, Stoops says, and a moment later an east basement window of the boiler room burst out. The boys peered in and saw the boiler enveloped in flames, which were also leaping along the roof of the room. They immediately turned in an alarm, and five lines of hose were in play within a short time.

DRAFTS FAN BLAZE
Central station firemen and spectators alike believed the blaze to be almost under control when, simultaneously with the sight of new flames leaping from the roof, crackling of fire could be heard at the west side of the church. Dense smoke soon covered the downtown district and opened doors and burned out sections of the roof. Fanned drafts through the building turned it into a roaring furnace.

Swelling crowds were kept back by fire lines as sturdy Kalamazoo firemen from Companies 1, 2, 4, 5, and 6, fought two hours to bring the blazes under control and protect the Y.M.C.A., First Baptist Church, and First Reformed Church, which, with the First Congregational edifice, covered the entire block bounded by Main, Church, Park, and Academy Streets. Part of the south wall, a section of the west roof, and a larger part of the east wall and roof crashed to earth. Though the tower remained intact, it was a brave and untiring crew of firemen who fought in armors of ice and frost to the end. Many of the men had to be released from jackets to remove their rubber coats after the fire.

PLANNED ENLARGEMENTS
Tuesday's disastrous fire upset extensive plans of the board of trustees to enlarge and repair the church, it is said. The board had several weeks before voted to spend a considerable sum to add another floor over the north and west wings of the church.

A meeting between the board and representatives of the firm of Billingham and Cobb, architects and engineers, had been set for 2:30 p.m. Tuesday. Draftsmen had been working on the plans and blueprints for the addition for two weeks. They had just been completed and were ready for final submission to the board of trustees when the church burst into flames.
