FRIDAY, MARCH 19, 1926

THE BEST REWARD FOR VALOR.

Whether or not the children of Claus Slater and John Morren will be given an education like other boys and girls is a question which the people of Kalamazoo must answer—and answer soon. The appeal made in their behalf by the local underwriters' club ought to receive a most generous popular response. We doubt if there were ten people in this entire city last Sunday morning who did not express, either audibly or to themselves, a noble desire to "do something" for the families of the two firemen whose unselfish devotion to duty cost them their lives in the First Methodist Church fire. The opportunity is now open to everyone who feels that such courageous conduct ought to be rewarded in the best possible way.

We think it is best to speak with perfect frankness on this matter. The fund of about $600 subscribed up to the present is altogether too small an amount to represent two days of campaigning in this worthy cause. The news stories published in The Gazette have stated plainly the conditions of the Slater and Morren families, and the public should have no difficulty in determining just how much is needed to make this movement a success. The three Slater children are all less than five years of age. The oldest one is supposed to start school next fall. The two Morren children are already in school, but they have many years to go before even their elementary education is complete. Unless the people of Kalamazoo give better monetary expression to their feelings of sympathy than has been evident thus far, the school careers of these five youngsters may be seriously endangered.

We trust that no one will be misled by the announcement, published yesterday, that "there is no immediate need of relief" for the Slater and Morren families. That simply means that the bereaved wives and children of the two firemen are not in perilous financial circumstances at the present moment. Their case, in other words, is not one for charity. And we may as well emphasize right now that the element of "charity" does not enter into the problem at all. The citizens who are sponsoring this movement are merely asking the people of Kalamazoo to make good on their obligation to Claus Slater and John Morren; to give assurance that the children of these heroic firemen will be given as good schooling as would have been theirs had their fathers not been forced to die in the service of the community.
