CORNERSTONE SERMON

During the laying of the cornerstone, the Rev. Waterman said:

"The new edifice to be built upon this cornerstone has been made a necessity. A church is and has always been the symbol and means of devotional objects and deserved development. It has become indispensable to the existence, stability, and efficiency of the body of believers. It is the real abiding place of the Holy Spirit. The need soon to be beyond the limit of this old house, the need for larger accommodations, the need for tools and workshops in good order and for furnishing which are to be used by ministers and their pupils."

"This material structure will soon decay into a mere dust but the truth of the Gospel will have a firm foundation. It will have a growth even greater than this important material framework."

An accompanying list of historical pieces placed in the cornerstone of 1839 showed that at that time there were 18 churches in Kalamazoo, one college, a female seminary, and a private boarding school. The population was about 2,000.

"The chief products are carts, carriages, and celery," the note stated. "The Hon. Otto Hilberg is the mayor of the city. The Hon. Cyrus Luce is governor of Michigan. The Hon. Benjamin Harrison is president of the United States."

"The laying of the cornerstone was attended by a large gathering of the congregation and citizens. This new structure will no doubt be one of the most convenient, commodious and elegant edifices in the state and the citizens should feel justly proud of their heroic undertaking. The exercises were characterized by simplicity with which our Pilgrim Fathers inaugurated the religious history of our country. All pastors of the city attended the ceremonies and gave brief addresses."

All papers and records removed from the cornerstone have been turned over to the Rev. Forrence T. Phillips, pastor of the church. They will be placed in the archives of the new church when its cornerstone is laid, it is planned.
