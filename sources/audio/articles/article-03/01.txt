SATURDAY, MARCH 14, 1926

THREE LINES OF HOSE BURST
Three different lines of hose burst during the fire, which further handicapped the firemen. These breakages were caused, Chief Russell stated, from excess pressure of the pumps.

Street car traffic was at a standstill during the fire, as power on the trolley wires had to be cut to make it possible for firemen to direct streams of water on the flames from the Rose Street side.

As night fell and the temperature lowered, the firemen became ice-coated and soaked from the spray of water. The streets around the church were flooded to a depth of one to three inches with water.

The walls of the church stood like ghostly spectres with a white coating of ice, and trees near the structure sparkled with frozen spray from the hose lines.

Frank Russell, who conducts a boarding house just east of the church, immediately opened his home to the firemen and prepared coffee and food for them. Other citizens sent sandwiches, and a number of city employees went to Central Station and prepared coffee, which was carried to the firefighters.

Four Kalamazoo Churches Ignited in Three Months
First Methodist Blaze Fifth Serious Visitation of Kind in City During Same Period

Saturday's blaze was the fifth bad fire which has occurred in Kalamazoo within the last three months. It was the fourth church fire in Kalamazoo during the same period.

On December 29, the First Congregational Church was destroyed by a fire which threatened to sweep several nearby structures. The loss was estimated at $175,000. It was partly covered by insurance. Two weeks prior to this blaze, the East Avenue M.E. Church was visited by fire which caused about $5,000 damage.

Closely following the Congregational Church blaze, the Knights of Pythias building on East South Street was swept by fire. The damage was estimated at $10,000. Scores of people in the building at the time the fire broke out were forced to leave by the fire escapes.

On January 26, the heart of Kalamazoo's business district was threatened when the Chase Block at Main and Rose Streets burst into flames shortly after 7:00 a.m. Firemen battled for nearly three hours with the stubborn fire, which virtually gutted the entire building, which was four stories high. The loss ran to more than $100,000.

Last Sunday morning's fire caused $5,000 damage to the Boudeman Building on South Burdick Street. The blaze started on the ground floor and had gained dangerous headway into upper floors when it was discovered.
