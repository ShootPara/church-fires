More hoses were called into play, and firemen climbed to points of vantage in an effort to head off the flames. By this time, however, the main auditorium of the church was in flames, and the holes which broke through the roof opened the disastrous draft which the firemen had hoped to prevent.

ESTABLISH FIRE LINES
Hundreds of people who were on the streets during the noon hour were attracted to the scene by the great volumes of yellow-black smoke which swept low into the streets in the business section, darkening the entire city. Fire lines had to be strung and the crowds were held back in safety, as it was feared the big walls might collapse at any moment. Firebrands dropped like snowflakes all over the business district.

The big brick tower of the church remained standing throughout the fire, although it made the work of the firemen extremely hazardous, as it was feared it might topple over at any moment.

TONS OF WATER USED
The flames, it is believed, swept through the entire building following the furnace pipes and ventilating system. The tons of water that were poured into the flames were believed to be sufficient to extinguish the blaze.

Fire engine companies No. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 responded with full apparatus, and No. 3 company from the East Side was drawn into Central station to stand ready for other emergencies.

BUILT IN 1890
The Congregational Church, which is the largest Protestant church structure in Kalamazoo, was erected in 1890, replacing the first church originally built on the same site nearly 50 years before. The contract for the new church was awarded to Glanfield and Son, builders, who came to Kalamazoo and secured, in rapid succession, contracts for erection of the original Y.M.C.A. building, the Congregational Church, and the People's Church.

The Congregational structure was ornate in design, with a tower at the southeast entrance. The exterior was largely of Marquette red stone, veneered around a heavy timbered interior. In addition to the church auditorium proper, the edifice contained a prayer meeting room, large parlors, kitchen, and pastor's study. The interior arrangement was such that many drafts and cross currents were formed, adding to the difficulty in fighting the fire.

The building, as it stands today, represents an outlay of about $175,000. It was built at a time when material and building wages were at the lowest price. Since the original construction, many minor changes have been made in the edifice, though in the main it stands today as originally planned.

The present pastor is the Rev. Torrance T. Phelps. The congregation numbers about 1,000 and includes in its enrollment many of the most prominent business and professional men of the community.

Before the fire was under control, the trustees of the People's Church formally offered use of their building to the Congregational Church as a temporary home.
