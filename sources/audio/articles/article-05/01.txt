WEDNESDAY, JUNE 2, 1926

FIFTH CHURCH FIRE CAUSES $500 LOSS
BURNING OF PEWS LAID TO CIGARETTE; SERVICES NOT TO BE INTERRUPTED

For the fifth time in as many months, fire Tuesday night visited a church in Kalamazoo. The blaze was fought for 30 minutes when it was extinguished, leaving a damage of $500.

The blaze started in the main lobby of the First Presbyterian Church, Rose and South streets, less than an hour after Masons had vacated the building after a banquet. A carelessly tossed cigarette stub is believed responsible.

Firemen and apparatus from stations 1, 4, and 6 responded to the alarm at 9:30. Smoke curled through the crevices between the large doors as firemen whined out their hose lines. The blaze was soon under control.

Several pews were burned or scorched, but there was no other damage from the flames, which were confined to the few square yards in which they originated. Most of the damage was from water thrown by the firemen.

The Presbyterian Church was threatened with destruction when a fire started in the motion picture booth several months ago. Tuesday night's church fire brought the number to five since last December when the East Avenue M.E. Church was damaged.

The second also occurred in December when the First Congregational Church was razed. A small fire in the Presbyterian Church was third, and the First Methodist fire last March was fourth.

Services planned for the church building will be carried on without interruption, it was stated Wednesday by officials after an inspection of the building.
