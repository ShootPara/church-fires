SUNDAY, MARCH 14, 1926

DISCUSS ORIGIN OF FIRE

Opinion is divided regarding the origin of the fire. Due to the fact that four different churches have caught fire within the last five months—two of them having been completely ruined, and also because several other disastrous downtown blazes have occurred during the last two months, opinions were voiced that the fire may have been of incendiary origin. Others were of the belief an overheated furnace or flue caused the fire, while there were still others who thought defective wiring may have started it.

Harry Oswald, city electrical inspector, stated that the system of wiring in the church was of old-type, but added the extensions which had been made to electrical apparatus in the church had been of late design.

The First Methodist church was the first to claim human lives since the disastrous Hall Brothers conflagration in 1907, when 10 firemen were killed. The fatalities at the church fire occurred after the fire had been subdued and the only task left to the firemen was the wetting down of the burning ruins inside the four walls.

The janitor of the church could not be located Saturday night, but it is believed he had attended the furnace fire late Saturday afternoon, to have the church warm for the Sunday services. Neither was it possible to ascertain the exact amount of insurance carried on the structure, although it was stated by certain members of the church that the insurance would total $50,000.
