FORMS RESCUE SQUAD
The first inkling firemen on the outside of the building had of disaster was when Leyden and others rushed out, shouting that their two companions had been buried beneath the falling roof. Chief Russell immediately organized a rescue party, and a dozen firemen and policemen, armed with axes, shovels, and lanterns, invaded the building, imperiling their own lives as portions of the roof and sections of the brick walls were still sliding to the main floor. No sign of the two firemen could be found at first.

Firemen, policemen, and officers from the sheriff's department began digging, throwing out the timbers and debris as fast as they could. They were severely handicapped for a moment by the high-powered streams of water that were still being played on the ruins and also by darkness. A big searchlight was wired up by Sheriff Borden and suspended over the workmen.

FIND FIRST BODY
The rescuers dug to a depth of one foot before any trace of either fireman was found. Then one shovel struck the leather-coated body of Slater. "I've found one body," informed a fireman. Then they started work with their hands, heaving away the bricks and other debris until half the buried fireman's body was uncovered. Slater was lying face downward. The firemen thought it would be but a few moments before the body would be unearthed, but it required exactly one hour and ten minutes to extricate the body of the first victim.

A huge timber had lodged across Slater's limbs, and firemen had to cut this away with axes. Care had to be used to avoid striking the body, and the firemen who were doing the work were in constant danger of plunging through the floor to the basement. It was necessary for three firemen to hold the body of Slater to keep it from sinking head-downward into the basement.

FELL FACE DOWNWARD
Finally, the body was freed and was carried to the H.S. Harrington morgue in the police ambulance. At that time, the body of Morren had not been found. Digging to the eastward of the place where Slater's body had been found, firemen located Morren's body, buried deep in the debris. He, too, had fallen face downward, having been struck just as he was about to pass through the doorway. Less difficulty was experienced in extricating Morren's body, and this gruesome task was completed in half an hour. His body was taken to the Harrington morgue.

CHIEF RUSSELL WEEPS
Tears coursed down the fire-blackened cheeks of Chief Russell, who had battled the flames for more than an hour and who had warned all his men to be careful in entering the building when the bodies of Slater and Morren were extricated from the ruins.
