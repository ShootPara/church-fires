WEDNESDAY, DECEMBER 30, 1925.

BURNED CHURCH TO BE REBUILT BY FALL OF '26
Preliminary Plans Call for Starting of Construction in Spring.


WALLS TO BE TORN DOWN
Services to Continue in People's Church; Flames Stop Plans for Enlarging Old Edifice.

On the site where this morning drab, skeleton-like walls stood as sentinels guarding the ruins of the First Congregational church, destroyed by Tuesday afternoon's $175,000 fire, a completely new and modern structure will rise. The congregation, undaunted by the colossal loss, will continue regular services in the People's church until the fall of 1926, when, it is expected, the new edifice will be ready for occupancy.

While weary and frozen firemen fought to save nearby buildings and to extinguish the smoldering wreckage of what had been Kalamazoo's largest Protestant church Tuesday afternoon, a joint meeting of the First Congregational church boards accepted the balance of fate with a spirit of optimism. The board of trustees and the advisory board members convened together and accepted an invitation to continue, without a break, regular services in the People's church. Immediate steps to build a new structure, complete and modern in every way, were taken.

PLANNING NEW CHURCH.

The joint board meeting members decided to appoint a building committee to formulate plans for a new First Congregational church and arrange to submit preliminary sketches for adoption by the congregation at an early date. Robert F. Staebler was named chairman of the committee consisting of L. W. Sutherland, Frank D. Fuller, Guy Wilson, W. O. Jones, A. B. MacDonald, and Donald S. Gillmore, who will work with the Rev. Torrance T. Phelps, pastor, in drawing up preliminary plans.

"Our church rises with optimism and energy to meet the urgent challenge," the Rev. Phelps, pastor, declares. "The walls of the old building, built in 1890, will be torn down and, if initial plans are carried out, work will start in early spring so that the new and more modern house of worship will be available by next fall."
