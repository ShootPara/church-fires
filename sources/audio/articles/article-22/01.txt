WEDNESDAY, JULY 7, 1926.

PRESBYTERIAN CHURCH FIRE LOSS $100,000
Third Downtown Religious Structure to Be Razed; Aggregate Damage $700,000.

BARE WALLS STANDING
Two Firemen Overcome by Smoke After Collapse of Roof.

Fire, believed to have been of incendiary origin, destroyed the First Presbyterian church building at South and Rose streets at 11:30 Tuesday night, with a loss estimated at $100,000 to $125,000.

Two firemen were partially overcome by smoke and gas fumes and seven others narrowly escaped injury or death when a portion of the roof fell to the street. The fire endangered a score or more downtown blocks.

TWO PREVIOUS FIRES

It was the third fire which had broken out in that structure within the last four months. The first fire, late in the winter, had started near a moving picture booth. The second blaze, which occurred during the Knights Templar conclave here in June, was discovered at 11:00 in the evening near the front end of the building. That fire did considerable damage to the front lobby, but was extinguished before it spread.

The Presbyterian church fire Tuesday night was the third big church blaze to occur in Kalamazoo this year. The First Congregational church, just one block from the ill-fated Presbyterian building, was the first large church to be completely destroyed. The First Methodist church fire, March 13, was the second major blaze and the one which claimed the lives of two firemen.

In addition to these three major church fires in which the destruction has been virtually complete, there have been smaller blazes discovered in places of worship—two of which were extinguished in East Avenue M. E. church, badly gutted in December, 1925, while the Church of Christ was visited by a small fire.

15 FIRES SINCE DEC. 16

The Presbyterian fire constituted the fifteenth big fire in Kalamazoo since Dec. 16, 1925. Of these 15 fires, 12 of them were in churches or in buildings in which fraternal organizations held meetings.
