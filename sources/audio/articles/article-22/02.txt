Among the buildings which were endangered by the Presbyterian church fire were:

The Pythian building, 10 feet east of the church.

Y.W.C.A. building, 30 feet north of the church.

Public Library, directly across the street.

Park club, on the southwest corner of South and Rose streets.

Odd Fellows temple, directly west of the Park club.

City hall, just west of the Odd Fellows' temple.

Post office, at Burdick and South streets.

Boudenman block, just east of the Pythian building.

A close watch was kept at all these buildings to warn the firemen if any portion of the roofs should be ignited by firebrands. Police and sheriff's officers established "beats" to and from these buildings. None of the surrounding structures were ignited, however.

Danger warnings were posted on the church building Wednesday morning by Building Inspector Alexander C. Leek, following an inspection of the ruins. The walls which are left standing are considered dangerous as are also the ruins which are piled high on the inside of the structure.

The fire, occurring at the time it did, held up street car traffic all over the city. Cars bound for their last run of the night were brought to a standstill when the power on all the lines was shut off and the long lines of hose were drawn across the tracks.

Every fireman and every piece of fire-fighting equipment was pressed into service with the outbreak of the fire. All reserve firemen were called on duty and remained on duty throughout the night. So dangerous did the fire become that the reserve truck, which is always kept at Central Station for emergency calls during a big fire, was rushed to the scene and kept in action.

Pumpers were located on every corner surrounding the church block and these powerful machines shot streams of water into the various hose lines at the rate of from 1,400 to 1,500 gallons per minute. Ten hose lines, eight shooting water directly into the building and two being manned from the Y.W.C.A. and Pythian building, were kept in operation continuously during the fire.

With the sounding of the general alarm, the Salvation Army, the stewards at the Odd Fellows' temple, and Mrs. Jerome S. Borden, matron at the County jail, prepared coffee, doughnuts, and sandwiches for the firemen.
