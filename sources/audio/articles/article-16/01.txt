WEDNESDAY, DECEMBER 30, 1925

FEARS WALLS MAY CRUMBLE.
City Building Inspector Andrew Leak, Tuesday afternoon warned the police to keep the building roped off to protect pedestrians passing along Academy and Park streets. The inspector fears the walls will crumble as the ice, now encasing the walls, thaws.

Dudley Luce of Lansing, deputy state fire marshal, in Kalamazoo Tuesday, witnessed the conflagration. He characterized the blaze as one of the most stubborn he had witnessed in several years.

“A few minutes after the fire got under way, it was apparent the church could not be saved,” he said. “The flames had too much of a start. Members of the Kalamazoo fire department should be complimented on their successful efforts to keep the flames confined to the one structure. The firemen are certainly smoke eaters. I did not see one of them driven back by the dense barrage that rolled from the building during the height of the fire.”

WARNING FOR JANITORS.
"I hope that Tuesday’s fire at the Congregational Church will serve as a warning to all janitors not to build ‘too hot a fire and then go away and leave it," Fire Chief Charles Russell announced Wednesday. “Tuesday’s blaze was the second church fire within the last month, and it is my belief that both started from the same cause.

"From all reports, Kalamazoo’s firemen did remarkably well in keeping the fire from spreading to other buildings. The church burned like a straw stack."

Chief Russell was unable to lead his men in Tuesday’s fire, being confined to his bed with neuritis, contracted several nights ago during the fire at the Roamer Motor Car Company coal sheds. He has been off duty for the last four days and is still too ill to answer alarms.

Two children, one a girl four years old, and a boy about five, were knocked down and drenched when one of the fire hoses broke away during the blaze. They were carried into the Y.M.C.A. building and were later taken to their homes. Neither was seriously injured.
