SATURDAY, JUNE 29, 1963

The Cornerstone Story
Downtown Churches Rebuilt Following 1925-26 Fires

By TERRY BROWN
Gazette Staff Writer

A visitor to Kalamazoo, wandering about Bronson Park, is inevitably impressed by the sight of churches bordering the park. He no doubt is even more surprised to see that three of the largest churches - First Congregational, First Methodist, and First Presbyterian - have cornerstones dated within a year of other. Cornerstones on the Congregational and Methodist churches read 1927; the Presbyterian, 1928.

The years were good years for church builders. The previous years, 1925 and 1926, were bad years for the Fire Department. Within six months in 1925 and 1926, the three churches were completely destroyed by fire.

The fires were part of a spectacular series of 15 (12 of which struck churches or fraternal lodge buildings) which plagued Kalamazoo in that six-month period. The fires were also responsible for the impressive churches which now surround the park.

THE Congregational Church was the first destroyed, on Dec. 29, 1925. It was the largest Protestant structure in town, constructed from the same red stone as the People's Church, and by the same contractor. Two boys going to the YMCA just past noon, noticed the windows cracking and buckling, and turned in the alarm. Within half an hour, the roof collapsed and any hope of saving the structure was gone.

Two and one-half months later the First Methodist Church burned down. The church, then located at the corner of Rose and Lovell, had been built in 1869. Even before the fire, property on the corner of Academy and Park was purchased for a new church. The fire began at 6 p.m. in the basement. Two firemen were killed.

After the Methodist fire, there was rumor of arson. A few weeks before, there had been a small fire in a projection booth in the Presbyterian Church. The Knights of Columbus headquarters and Moose Lodge also had been destroyed by fire. Sometime later there had been another small fire in the Presbyterian Church, this time in the lobby. There was little damage.

THEN on July 6 the First Presbyterian Church burned to the ground. The church, located on the corner of South and Rose (now the parking lot next to the YWCA), was, like the Methodist Church, about to be replaced anyway. The first was discovered near midnight and within an hour the roof had fallen. Firemen had to be content to save the Pythian Building (now the Park Building) and YWCA.

WITH THIS final blaze, people had little doubt arson was involved. The chief of police commented that circumstances indicated at least some of the blazes, were set. It was pointed out all 15 fires occurred in non-working hours - at noon, late evening, early morning or on holidays.

There was an investigating committee but no evidence of arson was ever found. Former fire chief Frank VanAtta was a member of the investigating committee. Contacted recently, he repeated that it was only "carelessness." The janitors, he said, were overworked - one church (the Congregational) was having a convention, and a cigarette or cigar probably started the blaze. "No arson," he said emphatically.

So the three cornerstones, dated within a year of each other, are products of a strange set of events. And the view from Bronson Park has been enriched by three of 15 fires which caused almost a million dollars of damage in a short six months.
