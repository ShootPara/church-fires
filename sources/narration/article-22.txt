WEDNESDAY, JULY 7, 1926.

PRESBYTERIAN CHURCH FIRE LOSS $100,000
Third Downtown Religious Structure to Be Razed; Aggregate Damage $700,000.

BARE WALLS STANDING
Two Firemen Overcome by Smoke After Collapse of Roof.

Fire, believed to have been of incendiary origin, destroyed the First Presbyterian church building at South and Rose streets at 11:30 Tuesday night, with a loss estimated at $100,000 to $125,000.

Two firemen were partially overcome by smoke and gas fumes and seven others narrowly escaped injury or death when a portion of the roof fell to the street. The fire endangered a score or more downtown blocks.

TWO PREVIOUS FIRES

It was the third fire which had broken out in that structure within the last four months. The first fire, late in the winter, had started near a moving picture booth. The second blaze, which occurred during the Knights Templar conclave here in June, was discovered at 11:00 in the evening near the front end of the building. That fire did considerable damage to the front lobby, but was extinguished before it spread.

The Presbyterian church fire Tuesday night was the third big church blaze to occur in Kalamazoo this year. The First Congregational church, just one block from the ill-fated Presbyterian building, was the first large church to be completely destroyed. The First Methodist church fire, March 13, was the second major blaze and the one which claimed the lives of two firemen.

In addition to these three major church fires in which the destruction has been virtually complete, there have been smaller blazes discovered in places of worship—two of which were extinguished in East Avenue M. E. church, badly gutted in December, 1925, while the Church of Christ was visited by a small fire.

15 FIRES SINCE DEC. 16

The Presbyterian fire constituted the fifteenth big fire in Kalamazoo since Dec. 16, 1925. Of these 15 fires, 12 of them were in churches or in buildings in which fraternal organizations held meetings.

The complete list of the major fires, which resulted in an aggregate loss of approximately $700,000, follows:

CHURCHES

East Avenue Methodist church (Dec. 17, the first of the series of church fires), $10,000.

First Congregational church (complete loss), $175,000.

First Presbyterian church (moving picture booth fire), $50.

First Methodist church (complete loss), $100,000.

First Presbyterian church (lobby fire), $250.

First Presbyterian church (complete loss), $125,000.

LODGE HALLS

Knights of Pythias temple (first fire in basement, small loss), $150.

Knights of Pythias temple (second fire, heavy loss), $10,000.

Chase block fire (Knights of Columbus quarters), $100,000.

Moose temple (housing nine different lodges), $100,000.

Trades and Labor Council hall, $10,000.

Herfendahl store (housing four lodges), $8,000.

OTHERS

Boudenman block, $10,000.

Sutherland Paper Company (paraffin plant), $25,000.

Macy’s garage, $20,000.

BUILDINGS ENDANGERED.

Among the buildings which were endangered by the Presbyterian church fire were:

The Pythian building, 10 feet east of the church.

Y.W.C.A. building, 30 feet north of the church.

Public Library, directly across the street.

Park club, on the southwest corner of South and Rose streets.

Odd Fellows temple, directly west of the Park club.

City hall, just west of the Odd Fellows' temple.

Post office, at Burdick and South streets.

Boudenman block, just east of the Pythian building.

A close watch was kept at all these buildings to warn the firemen if any portion of the roofs should be ignited by firebrands. Police and sheriff's officers established "beats" to and from these buildings. None of the surrounding structures were ignited, however.

Danger warnings were posted on the church building Wednesday morning by Building Inspector Alexander C. Leek, following an inspection of the ruins. The walls which are left standing are considered dangerous as are also the ruins which are piled high on the inside of the structure.

The fire, occurring at the time it did, held up street car traffic all over the city. Cars bound for their last run of the night were brought to a standstill when the power on all the lines was shut off and the long lines of hose were drawn across the tracks.

Every fireman and every piece of fire-fighting equipment was pressed into service with the outbreak of the fire. All reserve firemen were called on duty and remained on duty throughout the night. So dangerous did the fire become that the reserve truck, which is always kept at Central Station for emergency calls during a big fire, was rushed to the scene and kept in action.

Pumpers were located on every corner surrounding the church block and these powerful machines shot streams of water into the various hose lines at the rate of from 1,400 to 1,500 gallons per minute. Ten hose lines, eight shooting water directly into the building and two being manned from the Y.W.C.A. and Pythian building, were kept in operation continuously during the fire.

With the sounding of the general alarm, the Salvation Army, the stewards at the Odd Fellows' temple, and Mrs. Jerome S. Borden, matron at the County jail, prepared coffee, doughnuts, and sandwiches for the firemen.
