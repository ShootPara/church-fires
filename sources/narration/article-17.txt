THURSDAY, JULY 8, 1926

ANOTHER CHURCH FIRE
In the complete destruction by fire of the First Presbyterian Church edifice at Rose and South Streets, Kalamazoo loses another one of its most familiar landmarks. The large building destroyed Tuesday night was erected 42 years ago to replace the structure which had met a similar fate in 1883. While the passing of this venerable house of worship cannot be attended by a rather general feeling of regret throughout the city, there is some consolation in the knowledge that it probably would not have remained standing much longer anyway. The first unit of the new home of the First Presbyterian Church Society, on South Street, one block west of the old site, is rapidly nearing completion, and when finished it will be one of the most attractive works of architecture in Kalamazoo. It is unfortunate that the members of the Presbyterian congregation will be forced to undergo the inconvenience of holding their services in emergency quarters until their new edifice is ready for occupancy.

The First Presbyterian Church is the third large church building in Kalamazoo to have been demolished by flames within the last few months. The people of this city will long remember the First Methodist Episcopal Church disaster, in which two of our courageous fire fighters lost their lives. Sometime before that, the Congregational Church was burned beyond repair. Fire started twice in the Presbyterian building prior to Tuesday night but was successfully combated before any serious damage had been done. Lodge halls also appear to have been favorite victims of the epidemic of destructive blazes which have been sweeping this city. The rooms of the Knights of Columbus were utterly ruined by the conflagration which all but destroyed the Chase block; the Moose Temple on Portage Street suffered a heavy loss from the flames, and fires of less serious proportions have invaded the Knights of Pythias building twice and the Odd Fellows Hall on Main Street.

Of course, it is impossible to point to any definite evidence to the effect that a firebug has been operating in Kalamazoo. But it is equally impossible to show any other substantial cause for the repeated attacks by fire on lodge halls and church buildings. Because of their structure and the fact that they are unoccupied the greater part of the time, churches make easy "material" for the demented incendiary. If he can gain entrance, he can go about his destructive business unobserved, and flames, once started in such a building, make rapid headway. The lodge halls which have burned in Kalamazoo recently have all been situated in closely built downtown sections, and if the fires had not been checked as efficiently as they were, the resulting spectacles would have been such as to delight the eyes of a confirmed pyromaniac.

The three investigations which have been started by local and state authorities are quite in order.
