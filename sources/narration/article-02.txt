SUNDAY, MARCH 14, 1926

2 DIE IN M.E. CHURCH FIRE
TWO FIREMEN AND BURNING CHURCH IN WHICH THEY MET DEATH

The fire was discovered at 6:00 p.m. and, although the church is within a block of Central Fire Station, the entire roof was enveloped in flames before the firemen could reach the scene. Every available piece of fire apparatus in the city was thrown into action. All firemen off duty were called into service, and Kalamazoo's entire force of firemen were swiftly brought into service.

The fire is believed to have started in the south end of the basement of the church and was due either to an overheated furnace or to defective wiring, in the opinion of Fire Chief Charles H. Russell.

BATTLE FLAMES AN HOUR
For nearly an hour, the firemen battled with the flames from all sides, the blaze being too fierce for the men to enter the building. Thousands of tons of water were thrown into the flames, but the fire had gained such headway during the first few minutes that it was soon out of control. Frame buildings to the south and east of the church were saved from destruction by being drenched with a continuous downpour of water.

The fire was virtually under control when the tragedy which snuffed out the lives of two of Kalamazoo's most popular firemen occurred.

They had been sent in as a relief squad to firemen who had battled with the blaze from the start. Grasping a line of hose, Slater and Morren, in company with Firemen Ben Kruizenga, Martin Leyden, and others, entered the main entrance to the church to play water on the burning ruins inside.

They had reached the second doors, which open directly into the church proper, and had passed through the west doorway to a distance of about five feet.

As the three men were holding the line of hose, others, who had moved about six feet to one side, heard a crash above and noticed the roof of the second balcony—about 40 feet in the air—give way. They called to their companions to "run for your lives," and dashed to the door. Leyden, thought to have been the nearest to the door, had just crossed the threshold as the falling brick and timbers crashed to the floor. Kruizenga and one or two other firemen also reached safety, but not before falling brick had struck their feet.

Where two of the city's firemen met death when a balcony crashed down, burying them beneath tons of debris within the church.
ICE SAVES TWO HOUSES
A dozen streams of hose forced water on virtually every portion of the building, some wetting down the side walls, others crashing through the windows to the interior, and two playing on the home of Frank Russell, directly east of the church, and that of A.H. Rothermel, directly south of the structure. Fear was expressed for the safety of both these homes as the flames and firebrands were shooting in the direction of both. The Rothermel home is located about 10 feet from the rear of the church, while the Russell home is about a rod and a half away. Ice forming on the roofs of these two homes probably saved them from catching fire.

The church burned with great rapidity. Just one hour after the fire was discovered, the building was in total ruins. The roof had caved in, and nothing stood but the bare brick walls. Wooden cornices at the tops of the walls burned, and a fiery border circled the top. Luckily, none of the four walls collapsed, but policemen roped off fire limits to protect the thousands of people who thronged about the corner to watch the fire.

FIREMEN SEE BLAZE FIRST
Dense smoke clouded the entire neighborhood. Firemen at Central Station, just one block away, saw the smoke as it issued from the top of the structure and thus received the alarm. Just as the department was responding, an alarm came in from D.S. Field.

Every piece of fire apparatus in the city was pressed into service, and pumpers and trucks were stationed at every corner within a radius of one block from the church. Six pumpers forced a maximum of 6,000 gallons of water a minute on the fire.

Seven thousand feet of hose were put into use, more than a dozen lines being extended from the pumpers. Every fireman in the city, including all reserves, were called into service and fought the flames for more than three hours. Water was poured onto the burning ruins for two hours following the crashing of the roof.
