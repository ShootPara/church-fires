SUNDAY, MARCH 7, 1926

CHURCH CORNERSTONE YIELDS OLD BOX OF HISTORICAL DATA

Second Congregational church, erected in 1852, and (below), the Rev. W. A. Waterman, pastor at the time the structure recently burned was built.

Workmen employed in razing the First Congregational church, destroyed by fire last December, have removed its cornerstone containing interesting records and information concerning the erection of church and events which were taking place in Kalamazoo July 3, 1859, when the cornerstone was laid. The strong box containing these records was discovered by accident when a workman’s pick broke through the walls of the receptacle.

Photographs of the pastor, W. A. Waterman, then pastor of the church, the second Congregational church opened in Kalamazoo, and interesting views of the Kalamazoo during its early days were found in the cornerstone. Newspapers of the day and other dating back as far as 1859 were also in the box.

An old bible was also found among the old papers. It was presented by Deacon D. A. McNair in August, 1859. The church destroyed by fire was the third edifice built by the Congregationalists of Kalamazoo.

There were two hymn books, one presented by Roberts and Hillhouse, booksellers on 134 West Main street. There were a large number of church periodicals, letters of congratulation from ministers of other cities. The list of the church membership absorbed the interest of those looking forward in the mass of other interesting data.

FORMED JUNE 6, 1835 A historical sketch of the church disclosed that it was formed of 14 members June 6, 1835. It was then purely Presbyterian and continued as such until January 1841, when it adopted a rotatory session. In January 1842, it adopted the Congregational form of church government. It remained connected with the Kalamazoo Presbytery until June 1843, when it united with other churches forming the Kalamazoo Congregational Association.

The Rev. S. Woodbury was pastor from its formation until August 1839. The pulpit was vacant until 1840 when Rev. O. P. Hoyt was installed. Other ministers who served as pastor were the Rev. A. B. Keddie, Dr. A. T. Avery, Granville Walden, Ed. Ford, Taylor, Henry Mills, F. Brown, W. Lyman, Frank Boswell, William Thomas, E. Adams, Barrister, W. H. Hutt, Thomas Camp, W. H. Auch, William Gilland, Bissell, and other ministers.

In 1890 the present building was built at the cost of $48,000.

At a special meeting held April 25th, 1891, it was determined to build a new church to replace that which was destroyed in the last December. The cornerstone was laid with appropriate exercises, July 3, 1859, and the church was dedicated June 22, 1890.

Some of the contributors to the church building fund, ‘raised for the purpose of erecting, finishing, and furnishing a suitably commodious house of worship for the First Congregational church of Kalamazoo’ were: Mr. and Mrs. O. M. Allen, $1000; J. A. Brown, $1000; Julius C. Burroughs and wife, $400; Henry Beckwith, $200; H. Bird, $200; L. Cahill, $600; E. A. Crane and wife, $1000; C. W. Crooks, $100; Mrs. B. Clement, $100; S. A. Gibson, $1500; Gilmore Brothers, $300; L. Gates, $400; I. Hull, $100; Dr. Hudson, $6000; Dr. H. O. Hitchcock, $4000; D. H. Haines, $100; W. E. Hill, $200; Pennel Hobbs, $100; W. E. Hill, $200; H. P. Bird, $100; F. Crooks, $300; E. Bird, $200; S. A. Hudson, $6000; L. K. Bird, $100; Mr. Hudson, $100.

The girl's chorus included Vida L. Smith, Laura L. Burdick, Kate M. Belknap, Kittie C. Waterbury, Ida Bell Smith, Eva Mellen, Pauline Avery, and Edith M. Osborn.

Some of the old papers placed in the cornerstone included church periodicals and missionary magazines dating back as far as 1856. "There was a ‘Michigan Telegraph’ of June 3, 1852; Kalamazoo Gazette of Aug. 1, 1852; ‘De Nederlander’ of Aug. 6, 1852, which was Kalamazoo’s Holland-American newspaper edited by G. Van De Wall."

The cornerstone of the little brick church built in 1852 was laid Sept. 15, according to another brief message found in the strong box.

The letter read: "There are in this village at this date six Protestant churches and a small Irish Roman Catholic church. They include the ‘Calvinistic Baptist church with 176 members; Methodist Episcopal church with 80 members; St. Luke’s church with 60 members; Dutch Reformed church with 60 members; Presbyterian church with 150 members and the Congregational church with 169 members.

"There are four common schools which are intended to be consolidated into one Union school. There is also one and sometimes two private schools, also a female seminary and a Baptist Literary and Theological seminary, each in early stages of its growth, but promising."

"How changed will be the condition of this place (now numbering between 3,000 and 4,000 inhabitants), or the church when this shall be brought forth for perusal of some distant generation. May he then look down from the heights of heaven and see freedom and Christianity over spreading the earth."

This message was signed by A. S. Kedzie.

In another note A. S. Kedzie wrote in 1861, "It may not be amiss to preserve in this box a notice of the Ladies' Library Association. This body was formed in the winter of 1851. It is composed of ladies of all the different religious societies in this place. Each pays 25 cents a year for the use of the library. Gentlemen are entitled to the same privileges for one dollar a year."

"By library of about 150 very choice works have been obtained. No book is admitted unless approved by a library committee chosen for each church. A lecture is delivered before the association once each month. The ladies and gentlemen of each location spend one evening each month together for a literary social."

CORNERSTONE SERMON

During the laying of the cornerstone, the Rev. Waterman said:

"The new edifice to be built upon this cornerstone has been made a necessity. A church is and has always been the symbol and means of devotional objects and deserved development. It has become indispensable to the existence, stability, and efficiency of the body of believers. It is the real abiding place of the Holy Spirit. The need soon to be beyond the limit of this old house, the need for larger accommodations, the need for tools and workshops in good order and for furnishing which are to be used by ministers and their pupils."

"This material structure will soon decay into a mere dust but the truth of the Gospel will have a firm foundation. It will have a growth even greater than this important material framework."

An accompanying list of historical pieces placed in the cornerstone of 1839 showed that at that time there were 18 churches in Kalamazoo, one college, a female seminary, and a private boarding school. The population was about 2,000.

"The chief products are carts, carriages, and celery," the note stated. "The Hon. Otto Hilberg is the mayor of the city. The Hon. Cyrus Luce is governor of Michigan. The Hon. Benjamin Harrison is president of the United States."

"The laying of the cornerstone was attended by a large gathering of the congregation and citizens. This new structure will no doubt be one of the most convenient, commodious and elegant edifices in the state and the citizens should feel justly proud of their heroic undertaking. The exercises were characterized by simplicity with which our Pilgrim Fathers inaugurated the religious history of our country. All pastors of the city attended the ceremonies and gave brief addresses."

All papers and records removed from the cornerstone have been turned over to the Rev. Forrence T. Phillips, pastor of the church. They will be placed in the archives of the new church when its cornerstone is laid, it is planned.
