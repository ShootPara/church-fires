# Source review: Kalamazoo Church Fires presentation

Reviewed on 2026-11-11 from the mapped Google Drive folder and the native Google Slides/Docs files. This document is a migration brief, not a claim that every historical assertion has been independently verified.

## Executive description

The material is a substantial local-history presentation about a cluster of Kalamazoo fires between December 1925 and July 1926. Its emotional and historical center is the destruction of three major downtown churches, the deaths of two firefighters at First Methodist Episcopal Church, the unresolved public fear of a serial arsonist, and the congregations' rebuilding efforts.

The deck is not just a conventional lecture. It combines summary slides, a chronology, newspaper scans, long-form spoken article readings, historic photographs, source notes, custom 3D church reconstructions, rendered model spins, music, and an image appendix. The website should therefore work as both a guided story and a browsable evidence collection.

## Native Google material

### Presentation

The native deck is approximately 118 MB and contains 103 slides. Its persistent navigation vocabulary is `summary`, `timeline`, `deaths`, `investigations`, and `rebuilding`. Many slides are image-led and carry only a short church label—`FCC`, `MEC`, or `Pres`—because the surrounding narration supplies the story.

The opening establishes the claimed scale: 12 major church or meeting-hall fires between December 1925 and July 1926, losses above $700,000 (described in the deck as more than $22 million in 2024 dollars), and the destruction of First Congregational, First Methodist, and First Presbyterian churches. A detailed list also includes fires at East Avenue Methodist, Knights of Pythias, Knights of Columbus quarters in the Chase Block, the Moose Temple, the Trades and Labor Council hall, Herfendahl's store, Boudeman Block, Sutherland Paper Company, and Macy's garage. These totals and inflation conversions should be recalculated and sourced before publication.

The slide sequence then introduces downtown Kalamazoo and the buildings: Central Fire Station, the churches, the library, Bronson Park, the Marlborough Building, and the Majestic/Capitol Theatre. It uses historic streetscapes to situate the audience in a city adopting electricity and automobiles while retaining coal-fired heating and dense downtown construction.

The core chronology covers:

- First Congregational Church, Park and Academy Streets, destroyed at noon on December 29, 1925. Two boys, Arthur Stoops and John Triestram, noticed signs of fire and raised the alarm. Dense smoke, roof collapse, interior drafts, and concern for adjacent buildings dominate the contemporary account. The building was valued at $175,000 with $100,000 insurance in the transcription.
- First Methodist Episcopal Church, burned on March 14, 1926. The fire spread so rapidly that the roof was involved before crews from nearby Central Station could control it. Firefighters Claus Slater and John Morren were killed when part of a balcony/roof structure collapsed while they worked inside after the main fire appeared under control. The source material describes an enormous response, burst hose lines, frozen water, ice-coated walls, and aid supplied to exhausted firefighters by nearby residents.
- First Presbyterian Church, burned late on July 7, 1926. The fire was reported around 11:20 p.m. and appeared to begin in or near the pipe organ. Smoke, falling plaster, rapid roof failure, nearby occupied buildings, and strict orders against re-entry feature in the account. The congregation was already constructing a replacement church nearby.

The investigations section contains an important tension. Individual incidents were attributed or tentatively attributed to building-specific causes: the First Congregational origin was initially uncertain and sometimes associated with its heating plant; a committee concluded that the Methodist fire began at an overheated furnace/flue beneath the organ, not electrical wiring; the Presbyterian fire appeared to begin near its organ. At the same time, officials and newspapers openly suspected a `firebug` because of the targets, timing, repetition, and total losses. The website must preserve that uncertainty rather than claim that arson was proven for the whole series.

The rebuilding section shows proposed and completed replacement churches. First Methodist's 1929 building differed from Ernest S. Batterson's more ambitious rendering: its church house arrived later, in simplified form, and the proposed massive central tower was never built. First Congregational's sequence concludes with custom models and rendered media; one credit slide says the video, models, and music were made by Brian Ream, the script was by Brian/ChatGPT-4, and the original voice was OpenAI Ember.

Slides 76–103 form a historical-image appendix with titles such as Academy Street (1914), aerial South Burdick (1911), Bronson Park (1910), Bronson Park Fountain (1930), Burdick Street (1908 and 1910), Central Fire Station (1905), Elks Temple (1910), First Congregational (1910 and 1920), the library and Methodist church (1910), and Main Street (1923). Each image needs an explicit source and usage-rights record before redistribution.

### Verbatim article excerpts

`Article Text to be read verbatim` is a curated narration source for the three principal fires. It is not a full newspaper archive; it selects dramatic passages suitable for spoken delivery.

- The Congregational excerpt follows discovery by Stoops and Triestram, the basement attack, collapse of the west-wing roof at 1:05 p.m., citywide apparatus response, suffocating smoke, a dangerous tower, crowds, fire lines, and firebrands over downtown.
- The Methodist excerpt describes discovery around 6:00 p.m., the fully involved roof, deployment of the entire department, the fatal interior relief assignment, the collapse that trapped Slater and Morren, threat to neighboring homes, six pumpers delivering a stated maximum of 6,000 gallons per minute, roughly 7,000 feet of hose, burst lines, halted streetcars, freezing conditions, and civilians feeding firefighters.
- The Presbyterian excerpt describes the 11:20 p.m. discovery, rapid auditorium involvement, two firefighters nearly overcome by smoke, roof collapse, protection of the Pythian Building and Y.W.C.A., a near miss involving Chief Charles Russell and six firefighters, and Russell's order that nobody re-enter the unstable shell until dawn.

This document is the best base for faithful article narration. It should be preserved as source text, then normalized only for clear pronunciation, abbreviations, and transcription errors. Any editorial modernization should be visible in the transcript history.

### Gazette transcriptions

`Gazette Article Contents` is the largest textual source. It contains transcriptions and/or summaries corresponding to the 22 newspaper PDFs. Besides the three main fires, it covers:

- the first reports and next-day follow-up on First Congregational;
- uncertainty about its origin, hazardous ice-covered ruins, and interrupted expansion plans;
- the Methodist disaster, the fatal collapse, burst hose, demolition, and discussion of furnace versus wiring;
- the official overheated-flue conclusion for First Methodist;
- funeral honors and editorials for Claus Slater and John Morren;
- appeals and fundraising for their widows and six children, including a campaign framed as $1,000 per child;
- the First Presbyterian blaze, its earlier small fires, investigations, and plans to meet elsewhere;
- the suspected pattern across churches and lodge halls;
- discovery of the First Congregational cornerstone strongbox and its documents about the congregation and early Kalamazoo;
- rebuilding plans and later retrospectives.

The transcriptions are valuable but should be checked against the scans whenever wording, names, dates, dollar amounts, or quotations will appear on the public site. Some filenames and generated prose contain spelling errors, and the summary document includes interpretive language that should not be mistaken for the newspaper's own wording.

### Slide summaries script

`Slide Summaries Script` is an approximately 34,000-character narration plan. It converts the newspaper material into modern explanatory prose organized around slide roles such as summary, death, investigation, cornerstone, fundraiser, fire cause, and rebuilding. It is useful as a website-writing scaffold, but it contains AI-like concluding paragraphs and rhetorical generalizations. Treat it as a draft interpretation, not a primary source. The scans should not be dropped directly into the initial page load. Preserve the originals as archival downloads only if redistribution rights permit it; derive responsive article images or OCR-backed reading views for ordinary browsing. ## Recommended website translation

The evidence naturally supports these standalone sections:

1. **Opening overview** — a concise statement of the period, the three destroyed churches, total claimed losses, and the two firefighter deaths.
2. **Interactive but fully readable timeline** — dated events from December 1925 through July 1926; the underlying HTML remains complete without scripting.
3. **Three church chapters** — pre-fire building history, fire account, evidence gallery, cause/investigation, and what replaced it.
4. **The firefighters** — Claus Slater and John Morren, the collapse, public funerals, memorial language, and family relief campaign.
5. **Was it arson?** — side-by-side presentation of confirmed observations, official incident-level findings, newspaper claims, and unresolved questions.
6. **Rebuilding** — proposed designs, completed structures, and continuity of congregations.
7. **First Congregational visualizer** — images/video first, with optional local GLB viewing and reduced-motion fallbacks.
8. **Source library** — article metadata, corrected transcriptions, scans where permitted, audio, transcripts, image credits, and a methodology note.

## Immediate migration risks and decisions

- **Rights:** Newspaper scans, library photographs, and other borrowed images need redistribution and attribution review before they enter a public repository.
- **Repository size:** The roughly 716 MB source tree is unsuitable for an ordinary Git clone. Optimize derivatives and decide whether archival originals belong in Git LFS, GitHub Releases, or outside the public repository.
- **Historical certainty:** The existing narrative sometimes moves too quickly from contemporary suspicion to group-level arson language. Build a claim/source matrix.
- **Generated prose:** The summary script contains generic AI phrasing. Rewrite for a precise local-history voice.
- **Audio:** Regeneration should follow script approval, not precede it. Store transcripts and production metadata alongside each final clip.
- **Accessibility:** Newspaper images and visual slides need real reading alternatives, not captions that merely repeat titles.
- **Offline guarantee:** Production output must contain every runtime asset and pass a disconnected-browser smoke test.
